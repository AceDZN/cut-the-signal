# Twenty wrecks, four chapters

The campaign teaches one new kind of route decision at a time. Difficulty reflects signal dependency, direction, return length, checkpoint order and command pressure; raw room count is not a difficulty measure. These are authored layouts and graph rules, not random variants. Human playtesting should tune the curve.

## 01 · Learn the job (1–5)

Learn a safe cut and return; complete two jobs from a receiver; collect a chain from outside inward; read one-way arrows; cross an empty platform using an uploaded route. E assists with a legal return without using solution witnesses.

## 02 · Read the network (6–10)

Manage branch checkpoints, redundant electrical feeds, a dead-end dish, a valuable central receiver and a longer ring with empty crossings. Signal cables and walkways remain separate graphs. The quota lets a player keep selected useful checkpoints for a lower-value recovery in stages 1 and 9.

## 03 · Cross the dark (11–15)

Combine two loops and separate electrical feeds, then bridge several dark platforms. Stages 13–15 tighten the buffer to three commands: cut order and stopping at a live receiver become essential.

## 04 · Dismantle the wreck (16–20)

Shared feeds control several jobs at once. Plan north/south cascades, preserve a remote junction across three deep branches, and dismantle a ten-relay network with a three-slot buffer. The last stage requires every relay.

## Playability guarantees

Every level has a legal full-salvage witness, an independently searched quota and perfect solution, a rejected incomplete return, three progressive hints and a physical traversal test for every legal directed catwalk. Upload safety checks preserve the puzzle interpreter; U restores a prior trip if the player changes their mind. No witness is loaded into the normal player controls.
